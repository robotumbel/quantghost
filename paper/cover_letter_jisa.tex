\noindent Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \textbf{``Surviving Sanitization with
QuantGhost: Deployment-Robust and Stealthy Steganography in Neural-Network Weights,''}
for consideration as an original research article in the \emph{Journal of Information
Security and Applications} (JISA).

Pre-trained neural networks are increasingly shared through public model hubs, which
turns the model file itself into a supply-chain attack surface. Prior weight-steganography
attacks hide megabyte-scale payloads in float32 weights, but their survival under real
deployment has never been tested. We show, on real models, that the routine transforms a
production system applies---INT8 quantization, pruning, and fine-tuning---erase the
mantissa-bit payloads of StegoNet- and EvilModel-style attacks entirely, and that a
faithful spread-spectrum MaleficNet survives those transforms only at watermark-scale
capacity. Our paper asks, and answers, whether a payload can survive the full deployment
pipeline at high capacity while remaining stealthy and accuracy-preserving, and turns the
answer into both a fundamental limit and a concrete defense.

The manuscript makes the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A capacity--robustness--stealth trilemma.} For the function-preserving
  neuron-permutation group of convolutional and dense layers, we prove that no
  weight-embedding scheme can be simultaneously high-capacity, permutation-invariant, and
  stealthy-quantization-robust: any stealthy, quantization-robust, permutation-invariant
  channel is bounded by $\sum_\ell k_h^{(\ell)} k_w^{(\ell)}$ bits, a value our
  construction attains.
\item \textbf{QuantGhost.} We present a quantization-aware, distribution-preserving
  embedding that occupies the high-capacity, stealthy, quantization-robust corner of the
  trilemma. It survives INT8, pruning, and fine-tuning at $3.2$\,MB on a real ResNet-50
  for only $0.19$ percentage points of accuracy, and survives byte-exactly through
  PyTorch's \emph{production} INT8 kernel, not merely an analytical quantizer.
\item \textbf{A measured MaleficNet baseline.} Rather than quote prior art from its
  reported design, we re-implement MaleficNet and run it on the same INT8/pruning/
  fine-tuning pipeline. Its spread-spectrum payload does survive these transforms, but its
  recovery decays as $1/\sqrt{K}$ and reaches chance near one bit per weight, so it is
  transform-robust only at low capacity. This makes the trilemma concrete across two real
  constructions: only QuantGhost survives at high capacity.
\item \textbf{An attack and its detector.} Against the released Model X-Ray steganalyzer,
  QuantGhost is statistically indistinguishable from benign weights on a diverse model
  population where the prior attack is detected. We propose a targeted sub-quantum residual
  detector that reliably flags the basic attack across a narrow and a diverse
  $3000$-model zoo, while honestly showing that an adaptive attacker reaches chance on a
  realistic population, so detection must be backed by a structural bound.
\item \textbf{Limits of permutation defenses.} A faithful, full-model neuron permutation
  on a real ResNet-18, verified function-preserving to machine precision, removes the
  high-capacity payload; but a $196$-bit permutation-invariant channel survives and
  bootstraps recovery of an out-of-band, AES-encrypted payload, so permutation-based
  sanitization is incomplete.
\item \textbf{Generality and reproducibility.} We show the attack is architecture- and
  domain-agnostic on a real Vision Transformer and through a cross-dataset study on
  IoMT/IoT intrusion-detection models (CICIoMT, CICIoT), set out the requirements for a
  complete defense, and release the full code, data, and a one-to-one
  table/figure-to-script mapping as an open repository at
  \href{https://github.com/robotumbel/quantghost}{github.com/robotumbel/quantghost}.
\end{itemize}

We believe the work is a strong fit for JISA. The journal is a leading venue for
information hiding, steganography and steganalysis, and machine-learning security; it has
published closely related work, including the Model~X-Ray steganalyzer that our attack
evades and that we build our proposed detector against. Our manuscript contributes a
proven capacity limit, a new high-capacity deployment-robust attack, a measured comparison
with the strongest prior method, and a new detector, with direct relevance to the IoT/IoMT
deployments JISA readers study.

In the interest of full transparency, an earlier and substantially shorter version of this
work was declined without external review by JISA in June~2026. The present manuscript is
substantially revised and extended: it adds the capacity--robustness--stealth trilemma with
a rigorous proof, a measured MaleficNet baseline and capacity sweep, validation through a
production INT8 kernel, a faithful full-model neuron-permutation study, a proposed
steganalysis detector with an honest adaptive-attacker analysis, a Vision Transformer and
cross-dataset generalization study, and a uniform end-to-end metric accounting. We submit
it in that light and defer entirely to your judgment on its suitability.

This research is offensive in nature but carried out to strengthen defenses. All payloads
in our experiments are encrypted random bytes or an AES key; no live malware is embedded,
executed, or distributed. We will responsibly disclose our findings to the authors of the
affected detector and defense (Model~X-Ray and NeuPerm) prior to publication, and the
paper proposes concrete mitigations.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not
under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have read and approved the
submission and declare no competing interests.

Thank you for considering our work. We look forward to the reviewers' comments.
